Issue 1: LLM would utilize local machines instead of tapping into the network
I realized that as we were doing rounds of testing, the answers to each prompt came back unnaturally fast. As I was checking the supplier endpoint to figure out which machine the prompt was running on, I saw that it showcased my own computer. Going back to the spec.md, it is clearly stated that our project is supposed to get our prompt answered on external machines running their local LLM. To double check this, I asked Codex to see if there were conditions regarding which machine they would look at, and when it answered no, gave Codex a set of conditions to which to specify when shooting the prompt.

Issue 2: Codex would run for 20 minutes to generate complete and complex lines of code with no traceability
As we were slowly developing the logic behind the system, we’d tell Codex our end to end goal and continuously update the spec.md. However, Codex would often times scan through our inputs and spec.md, and immediately generate long lines of codes without breaking down how that would work. We had to often times backtrack and tell Codex to break it down step by step and explain what was being added.

Issue 3: The generated steps is not exactly the most intuitive
Codex would also create multistep processes that would follow through with the logic but be overly complicated to execute on our laptops. During one of our iterations, Codex would generate almost a multi step process on how to get our LLMs downloaded and running, connect to the router, register as a supplier endpoint or so. From an interactive perspective, we were not sure which step led to what and it became overly complicated, with us needing to restart the process often as we’d encounter issues halfway through such as getting the wrong QWEN model, or even getting Ollama setup in the background. Interacting with how the software ran gave us a better idea on how to make it more user friendly, how to simplify the step processes, and forced us to figure out the logical flow before execution.
